## Icon foundations

### What is an icon

An icon is a simplified graphic symbol that represents an object, action, or idea. Unlike an illustration (which tells a story) or a logo (which is a part of a brands identity), an icon’s primary job is utility. It is a functional tool, a simplified visual image providing universal usage, which helps people understand information quickly. It can guide, inform or trigger a response.

### Why do we use icons

While GOV.UK has traditionally relied on a minimalist, text-first approach to minimise cognitive load, we recognise that iconography can be a powerful tool for accessibility. Icons have a unique ability to bridge comprehension gaps, particularly for low-literacy users, and when paired with text, they act as a vital visual anchor. Moreover, on devices and channels with limited screen space, icons can communicate meaning, provide visual cues and support wayfinding where longer text may not be practical.

However, we urge designers to use icons sparingly and with purpose. Overusing icons can create visual clutter, making pages harder to scan and increases cognitive load for users.

## Principles

Our iconography is rooted in the visual heritage of the UK government, evolved to feel warmer and more approachable. Our style balances a friendly, rounded exterior, referencing the 'dot' motif, with sharp internal details. This contrast ensures our icons feel both human and highly functional, blending accessibility with professional efficiency.

These principles are listed by priority. Icon design is rarely an exact science, and it isn't always possible to satisfy every principle at once. These are ranked to help you make the right trade-offs when goals conflict.

### Purposeful

Icons should only be introduced when they demonstrably simplify a task for users or improve the speed of recognition.

### Consistent

Icons must strictly adhere to our visual construction rules and utilise clear, intuitive metaphors that remain consistent across all government channels. Discussed in the [visual system](/icons/visual-system) section of the guidelines.

### Universally understood

Icons should communicate effectively across a global audience. Use widely recognised conventions and be mindful of cultural differences. Clear, culturally neutral visual shorthand helps overcome language barriers, reduces user friction, and makes interfaces more intuitive and inclusive for everyone.

### Instantly readable

Icons must be instantly understood allowing users to process meaning at a glance without interrupting their journey.

### Quietly recognisable

The GOV.UK icon style is humble but present. It uses a unique visual grammar to balance warmth with utility, ensuring every symbol feels authentic to the government’s heritage rather than following trends.

Choose the right visual asset based on the complexity of your message and the constraints of the platform. Standard icons support simple, functional tasks, while enhanced icons are more detailed versions used to draw attention, support wayfinding, or reinforce key messages when there is not enough space for a full illustration. See the [Usage Guidance](/icons/usage-guidance/) section for more information.

This distinction is especially vital for web and app interfaces, which should remain lean and functional, whereas full illustrations are reserved for complex editorial concepts and high-impact communication where the layout allows for greater visual depth.
